What you need

  • Confluence Cloud.
  • Lexiwright installed on your site. Installing an app needs a Confluence site administrator.

Nothing has to be installed in anyone's browser. Lexiwright reads and changes pages as the person using it, so Confluence permissions apply. The first time an app is used, Atlassian shows a consent screen. Approve it once.

Start a glossary in four steps

  1. Open Apps and choose Glossary.
  2. Select Add a term. Fill in the Term, its Synonyms and abbreviations (separated by commas) and the Definition, then select Save term.
  3. To bring in a whole list at once, use the import on the settings page (see Settings).
  4. Open any page. Its byline shows Glossary, with the number of glossary terms the page uses.

See the terms a page uses

Every page shows Glossary in its byline. When the page uses glossary terms, it says how many, for example Glossary: 9 terms.

  • Select it to list the terms with their definitions. Deprecated terms come first, with the term to use instead.
  • Open the glossary takes you to the whole glossary.

Terms are matched as whole words:

  • A term matches its synonyms and abbreviations too, and a one-word term also matches its plural (invoice, invoices).
  • Between the words of a multi-word term, a space, a hyphen or a slash all match.
  • Where terms overlap, the longest one wins.
  • A term marked Match exact upper and lower case matches only with the same case, so that API is not confused with api.
  • Names and synonyms are unique across the glossary, so a word never points to two definitions.

Suggested terms are not matched until an editor approves them.

Link glossary terms in a page

  1. Open the page, open the ••• menu and choose Link glossary terms….
  2. The dialog lists the glossary terms found in the page and how often each occurs. Approved terms are ticked; deprecated terms are not. Terms that already have a glossary link in the page are marked Linked.
  3. Select Link N terms.

The first time each chosen term appears in the body text, it becomes a glossary link. Click it to open the definition. Headings, links, code and other macros are not changed.

The page is saved as a new version by you, so you need permission to edit the page, and the page history keeps the version from before. Up to 200 terms can be linked in one go.

Macros

Type /glossary in the editor and choose one of the two macros.

  • Glossary term shows one term in the text, and a click opens its definition. In its settings, choose the Term. Under Text in the page, type another form of the word if the sentence needs it, for example a plural.
  • Glossary list shows the glossary, or one category, from A to Z with the definitions. In its settings, choose a Category (leave it empty for all terms) and whether Deprecated terms are hidden or shown with a label.

The glossary

Open Apps, then Glossary.

  • Search names, synonyms and definitions, browse from A to Z, and filter by Category and Status. Results come 50 at a time.
  • Open shows a term with its definition, synonyms, category, owner, link and, for a deprecated term, the term to use instead.
  • Editors use Add a term, Edit, Approve and Delete.
  • When suggestions are on, everybody else uses Suggest a term. A suggestion waits under the status Suggested, to review until an editor approves it.

The fields of a term

FieldNotes
TermUp to 80 characters. Names are unique across the glossary
Synonyms and abbreviationsUp to 10, separated by commas. Pages are matched on the term and on each of these
DefinitionUp to 2,000 characters
CategoryFree text, for example Finance
OwnerA team or role, for example Data governance. Free text, not a user account
Read moreAn optional link that starts with https:// or http://
Match exact upper and lower caseFor abbreviations such as API or IT
StatusApproved, Suggested or Deprecated. A deprecated term can name the term to use instead

Settings for administrators

As a Confluence administrator, open Settings, Manage apps, find Lexiwright and choose Configure. This opens the settings page Lexiwright glossary with three tabs.

Permissions

  • Who can add, change, approve and delete terms: Everyone who can use Confluence, or Only these groups (and Confluence administrators) with the Editor groups you choose.
  • Others can suggest terms: everybody who may not edit can suggest terms, and editors review them before they appear.

Everybody who can use Confluence can read the glossary. Linking terms in a page needs Confluence's permission to edit that page.

When editing is limited to groups, Confluence administrators are recognised by membership of one of the groups site-admins, confluence-admins, confluence-administrators, administrators or org-admins.

Import

Paste a CSV file (comma, semicolon or tab separated). The first line names the columns:

term, definition, synonyms, category, status, owner, link, match_case

Only term and definition are required. Choose Add new terms and update terms with the same name, or Replace the whole glossary (administrators only). Check the file lists every problem by line, including name clashes, before anything changes. Then select Start import.

Export

Create the export gives the whole glossary as CSV text in the same columns as the import. Select all, copy and save it as a .csv file. It can be imported again on this site or another one.

Rovo (optional)

If your site uses Atlassian Rovo, the Glossary agent answers questions such as "What does SLA mean here?" from your glossary, names the term to use instead of a deprecated one, and says so when a term is not in the glossary. It uses the Look up a glossary term action, which reads only the glossary. Suggested terms are not used.

Limits

  • Terms are not highlighted all over a page by themselves: Forge apps cannot change how a page is drawn. The byline list and Link glossary terms do that job.
  • Pages only. Blog posts are not scanned or linked.
  • A term that is bold in the text loses its bold when it is linked.
  • Up to 3,000 terms, 2,000 characters per definition and 10 synonyms per term.
  • The export is CSV text to copy, not a file download.
  • Without an active licence, the glossary, the byline and the macros show no glossary content. The terms stay stored.

Permissions and data

Lexiwright keeps the glossary and its settings in Forge storage on your site and stores no account ids. It reads and changes pages as the person using it. See Security and the Privacy Policy.
