What it does

Every organisation has its own words: product names, abbreviations, process terms. When they live in a spreadsheet or in someone's head, new colleagues guess, pages contradict each other and the same question comes back every week. Lexiwright keeps the words in one glossary in Confluence and brings the definitions to the pages where people read them.

Every page shows in its byline how many glossary terms it uses, and the definitions are one click away. Nothing has to be installed in anyone's browser. Editors approve, suggest and retire terms, and the first occurrence of each term in a page can be turned into a link to its definition.

  • Terms on every page

    The page byline says how many glossary terms the page uses. One click lists them with their definitions, synonyms and abbreviations. Nothing is installed in anyone's browser.

  • Link terms in one step

    Choose the terms, and the first occurrence of each becomes a glossary link that opens its definition. Headings, links and code stay as they are, and the page history keeps the old version.

  • Matching you can trust

    Whole words, synonyms, abbreviations, plurals and hyphenated forms. Mark an abbreviation case-sensitive so that API is not confused with api. A word never points to two definitions.

  • A governed vocabulary

    Approved, suggested or deprecated, with the term to use instead. Choose who edits by group, let everyone else suggest terms, and review the suggestions in the glossary.

  • Import and export

    CSV import with a preview that lists every problem by line before anything changes, and an export in the same columns.

  • Macros and Rovo

    An inline Glossary term macro, a Glossary list macro (A to Z, or one category), and an optional Rovo agent that answers from your glossary.

Built only on Atlassian Forge

Lexiwright is built only on Atlassian Forge. Its manifest allows no outbound network access and has no remotes, no web triggers and no Connect modules. It makes no calls to outside servers, and your data stays in Atlassian: in Confluence itself and in Forge storage on your site. Its eligibility for Atlassian's Runs on Atlassian program has not been confirmed yet.

Good to know

  • Terms are not highlighted all over a page by themselves: Forge apps cannot change how a page is drawn. The byline list and Link glossary terms do that job.
  • Pages only. Blog posts are not scanned or linked.
  • Linking writes a new page version as the person who links, so that person needs permission to edit the page. A term that is bold in the text loses its bold when it is linked.
  • Up to 3,000 terms, 2,000 characters per definition and 10 synonyms per term.
  • The export is CSV text to copy, not a file download.
  • Without an active licence the glossary, the byline and the macros show no glossary content. The terms stay stored.

Data and security

Lexiwright runs entirely on Atlassian. The glossary is kept in Forge storage on your site, pages are read and changed as the person using the app, and it makes no calls to outside servers. The security page lists what is stored, for how long and which permissions are needed. The privacy policy and the EULA are public.
